# V22 Story Architecture

## Why this exists

The Hub should not be designed as a collection of pages or as one master diagram. It is one knowledge product with one semantic/source model and several projections of that model.

The current nine golden-scene prototypes reveal a useful structure that was not obvious before rendering: the strongest directions naturally separate into three reader intents.

- **Understand** — authored explanation of the governing mechanism.
- **Explore** — spatial / interactive reasoning over relationships, conditions and consequences.
- **Inspect** — analytical access to exact objects, sources, versions, evidence and status.

These are reader states, not job titles. An executive, risk lead, product manager or builder may move between all three.

## What the cross-project history says must remain true

Across the Hub work, the stable requirements are:

- begin with useful CLM work and decisions, not AI architecture;
- Kaicy is one example, not the organising principle;
- Source of Wealth is one worked specimen, not the Hub identity;
- AMLR and FenX are important proof paths, not the whole product;
- trusted data, governed knowledge/methods, integration/conditions, human responsibility and evidence must remain visible;
- requirements work should connect authority → interpretation/instruction → expected behaviour → implementation/test/evidence without collapsing those authority classes;
- current, proposed, historical, illustrative, missing and uncertain states must remain explicit;
- reuse is a separate review question, not an automatic consequence of a successful task;
- a live runtime, interface, permission and production approval are different conditions;
- the visual bar is the supplied/generated reference set: authored composition, material differentiation, scale variation, strong information graphics and a calm analytical identity.

## What the current 1920×1200 renders teach us

The nine golden scenes are technically sound and materially more authored than the previous V22 runtime. They also expose a new opportunity: A/B/C should not be treated only as three aesthetic competitors.

Some directions are better understood as different projections of the same semantic core.

### Overview

- **Understand seed — B / The annotated work desk.** Strongest editorial composition. Context, intent, prepared work and review coexist as authored artifacts.
- **Explore seed — A / The working landscape.** Best mechanism map. Three representative work routes share context, hit a human-review seam and expose a separate reuse route.
- **Inspect/compare seed — C / The work score.** Useful aligned comparison of Run / Develop / Govern-change, but visually too sparse to lead the product. Treat as an analytical projection, not the hero.

### Knowledge

- **Understand seed — C / The context aperture.** Strong memory handle: not all available knowledge is context for every task. The review aperture makes selection and permitted use visible.
- **Explore seed — B / The exploded record.** Makes meaning, scope and provenance simultaneously inspectable while preserving one identity.
- **Inspect seed — A / The annotation loom.** Best source-to-structured correspondence view; useful when exact text, structured meaning and consumption boundary need to be checked together.

### Change

- **Understand seed — B / The version fault line.** Strong governing distinction: historical evidence remains on its side of the version boundary.
- **Explore seed — A / The impact field.** Best propagation reasoning: one changed proposition creates candidate downstream review paths without implying automatic update.
- **Inspect seed — C / The proposition ledger.** Best line-by-line consequence view; appropriate for exact review rather than orientation.

This does not mean all nine directions pass the final visual bar. Several remain too pale, too sparse or too text-led compared with the reference atlas. It means the experiment has discovered useful native modes that should inform the next iteration.

## Governing thesis

**AI becomes useful at scale in CLM when real work is grounded in explicit context, bounded by human responsibility and evidence, and leaves behind reviewed knowledge that can travel to the next task under known conditions.**

This is a mechanism claim, not a maturity claim. It does not assert that the target state is implemented today.

## Reader success contract

### Understand

After the guided story, a reader should be able to explain:

1. what kinds of CLM work AI can assist with;
2. why task context matters as much as model capability;
3. where human judgement or authorised action remains;
4. why evidence and operating conditions change what is possible;
5. why useful work does not automatically become reusable institutional knowledge.

### Explore

After exploration, a reader should be able to:

1. trace which context informs a selected task;
2. inspect typed relationships between sources, requirements, methods, tests and evidence;
3. vary a condition or revision and see what changes versus what remains fixed;
4. follow candidate impact without confusing dependency with automatic propagation;
5. move from a story into the exact related objects.

### Inspect

After inspection, a reader should be able to answer:

1. what exactly is this object and which version/status does it have;
2. what source or interpretation supports it;
3. which relationships are established versus proposed;
4. what evidence exists and what is missing;
5. what would need review before reuse, implementation or authoritative action.

## Three core stories

The stories are projections over the canonical model. They must reference existing object/source identities where possible rather than create a parallel knowledge graph.

### Story 1 — Useful work first

**Claim:** AI contribution in CLM is useful only in relation to a defined task, its context, its reviewer and its operating conditions.

**Memory handle:** `Work → context → contribution → review → evidence → reuse`.

**Story beats:**

1. **Start with work.** Run CLM, Develop CLM and Govern/change CLM need different work verbs and produce different reviewable artifacts.
2. **Expose context.** Rules/instructions, process/system knowledge, methods and evidence inform the task; they are not generic model memory.
3. **Bound the contribution.** Retrieve/structure/prepare; analyse/draft/test; compare/trace/challenge. AI prepares work rather than acquiring institutional authority.
4. **Show the review seam.** The responsible colleague checks meaning, suitability and next action. The seam is part of the work, not a final governance sticker.
5. **Vary operating conditions.** Supplied material may support bounded work while live information, shared operation or record change require different interfaces, permissions and support conditions.
6. **Separate reuse.** A useful output may yield a candidate instruction, method or context object, but the next use needs applicability, ownership, maintenance and permitted consumption.

**Explore handoff:** open the working landscape focused on a selected work route.

**Inspect handoff:** open the exact task artifact/source/object used in the example.

### Story 2 — Meaning that can travel

**Claim:** Knowledge becomes reusable when meaning, subject, provenance and scope stay attached and a tested consumption route exists.

**Memory handle:** `Meaning stays attached.`

**Story beats:**

1. **Read the source.** Begin with the actual meaning and source scope, not a decontextualised field/value.
2. **Make context explicit.** Add subject, version, field meaning and relevant constraints without upgrading authority.
3. **Keep the distinctions.** Structured does not mean applicable; applicable does not mean executable; document presence does not mean skill availability.
4. **Bind a method to a task.** A method uses selected context and inputs to produce a reviewable output with explicit gaps.
5. **Use a review aperture.** Selection is deliberate. Not all available knowledge belongs in every task, and permitted consumption matters.
6. **Review the next use.** Reuse carries its own scope, owner, source/version and maintenance questions.

**Explore handoff:** open the exploded record or context aperture with selectable meaning/scope/provenance.

**Inspect handoff:** open source-to-structured alignment for the selected instruction/context object.

### Story 3 — Change creates reviewable consequences

**Claim:** A changed instruction or interpretation creates impact-review candidates and new evidence needs; it does not automatically rewrite downstream behaviour or historical evidence.

**Memory handle:** `Change creates questions before it creates truth.`

**Story beats:**

1. **Name the changed proposition.** Make the semantic delta explicit rather than treating a document revision as one opaque object.
2. **Trace candidate impact.** Requirement, mapping, method and test may need review. Dependency is not automatic update.
3. **Compare expected behaviour.** Show precisely what would need to change and what remains unchanged.
4. **Keep historical evidence pinned.** Previous evidence retains the version/scope it originally covered.
5. **Expose the evidence gap.** A revised proposition needs relevant new acceptance evidence; an empty or proposed test is not a pass.
6. **End at a human decision.** Confirm meaning/applicability, responsible reviewers, implementation intent and evidence plan before controlled content changes.

**Explore handoff:** open the impact field at the changed proposition.

**Inspect handoff:** open the proposition ledger or evidence object at the exact version boundary.

## Lenses over the same stories

Do not create a separate website per initiative. Use lenses only where the canonical model contains enough evidence/context.

1. **Run CLM / preparation** — source-linked preparation, field meaning, method, review and missing information.
2. **Develop CLM / requirements & testing** — instruction → requirement → mapping → adverse test → evidence.
3. **Govern/change CLM** — changed source/interpretation/instruction → candidate impact → decision/evidence boundary.
4. **Reuse / shared context** — candidate artifact → applicability/ownership/maintenance → bounded consumption in another task.

Named systems such as FenX or Kaicy may appear inside a lens when source-backed and relevant; they do not become global navigation or strategy labels.

## Story scripts should declare reasoning intent, not diagram type

A story step should say:

- claim;
- reasoning operation (`trace`, `compare`, `challenge`, `diagnose`, `inspect`, ...);
- focus object IDs;
- what should become visually salient;
- what must remain fixed;
- false-inference risk;
- handoff target.

Do not make authors choose `graph`, `swimlane`, `heatmap`, etc. before the reasoning need is clear. Renderer families are extracted after successful scenes, not prescribed in advance.

## Interaction thesis

Interaction should make a relationship, condition, boundary or consequence easier to understand.

Strong interactions for this Hub include:

- select a work route and see context/output/reviewer change together;
- select meaning/scope/provenance and keep one object identity stable;
- remove one context element and see a task packet become visibly incomplete;
- introduce a revision and see only candidate impact paths change while historical evidence stays fixed;
- toggle an operating condition and see one route open/close without granting unrelated permissions;
- move from a story scene directly into the exact source/object/evidence record.

Avoid interaction whose main result is visual novelty.

## Design-Lab split

Keep the existing production Design Lab as the **Semantic Acceptance Lab**. It is good at invariants, stress tests and regression thinking.

Enhance the composition prototype as the **Narrative / Visual Lab**. It should answer:

- Does the story produce the intended mental model?
- Which projection best fits Understand / Explore / Inspect?
- Which illustration actually reduces inference cost?
- Does the interaction teach something?
- Does the scene still stand beside the reference atlas visually?

The two labs should eventually cross-check each other, but do not collapse them into one dashboard.

## Next design move

Do not build a generic story engine yet.

Use the three stories above to revise the strongest golden scenes and test the mode handoffs. Once the three story experiences work, extract the minimal story schema and renderer grammar from the successful compositions.

# V22 Reference Atlas — what excellent looks like

## How to use this atlas

This is not a mood-board dump and not a style-copying brief.

Each reference has a **specific lesson**. During composition work, use only references relevant to the scene being designed. The attached/generated Hub boards remain the primary internal visual bar; their generated facts, counts, statuses and enterprise claims are not factual authority.

For each golden-scene iteration, capture the relevant external reference at desktop scale and place it beside the 1920×1200 render. Record only the lesson being borrowed and the pattern being rejected.

## Reference hierarchy

### Tier 1 — direct art-direction comparators

These should influence composition most strongly.

#### A1 — Supplied/generated CLM AI Hub boards

**Role:** primary visual-quality bar.

**Learn:** authored hierarchy; layered document/material objects; contextual annotations; clear focal scene; varied scale; source/knowledge/work/evidence represented with different geometries; deep green used selectively; dense and sparse zones coexist.

**Do not copy:** generated facts; article numbers; owners; approvals; evidence counts; fake freshness; unsupported status; source-of-wealth as the Hub ontology.

**Use for:** all three golden scenes.

---

#### A2 — Initiate Architecture

Source: https://initiatearchitecture.com/

**Capture target:** homepage first viewport and the interactive transformation area.

**Learn:** treat the whole viewport as a compositional canvas; oversized hierarchy; asymmetry; large fields of unboxed content; interaction integrated into the visual idea rather than added as controls; confidence in leaving space unused.

**Do not copy:** architectural brand identity, orange palette, commercial calls to action, literal imagery.

**Best for:** Overview / How AI fits into CLM work.

---

#### A3 — pen.dev

Source: https://www.pen.dev/

**Capture target:** first product composition and canvas/product sections.

**Learn:** visual canvas and code should be one workflow; large visual planes can carry the explanation; diagrams and repeated/data-driven structures may be generated in code; agents benefit from manipulating a compositional artifact rather than only styling final DOM.

**Do not copy:** creator-tool marketing, neon/tech novelty, unrelated 3D effects.

**Best for:** composition process; Knowledge; Change.

---

### Tier 2 — analytical product / workspace references

These should influence inspectable product surfaces, not the editorial opening.

#### B1 — EigenPal

Source: https://www.eigenpal.com/

**Capture target:** product/workflow sections showing human checkpoints, logged work and evaluation.

**Learn:** trust is visible in the work surface; human checkpoints, reversibility, evidence and evaluation belong inside the workflow; serious document work can remain visually modern without hiding control boundaries.

**Do not copy:** autonomous-agent language or claims not supported for CLM.

**Best for:** Change, Evidence, source/object inspection.

---

#### B2 — Lokalise project editor / screenshot context

Sources:
- https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/2089277-project-editor
- https://docs.lokalise.com/en/articles/2045882-screenshots

**Capture target:** editor with contextual controls and screenshot-linked context.

**Learn:** keep the interpreted object beside its context; preserve simultaneous inspection; let an object carry links, screenshots/comments/metadata without sending the user through sequential pages; context can be directly addressable.

**Do not copy:** translation-specific density, toolbar complexity or enterprise feature chrome.

**Best for:** Source reader, Knowledge object, search/result preview, Develop CLM.

---

#### B3 — Cohere North

Source: https://cohere.com/north

**Capture target:** large product-surface sections and capability framing.

**Learn:** one large product surface can carry more credibility than many explanatory cards; disciplined whitespace around complex software; capability groups can be distinct without becoming a taxonomy wall.

**Do not copy:** AI-platform marketing, generic agent centrality, claims of automation/security/readiness.

**Best for:** Overview and deeper analytical workspace transitions.

---

#### B4 — Invisible Technologies / How we work

Source: https://invisibletech.ai/how-we-work

**Capture target:** workflow + validation/product screenshot sections.

**Learn:** real work can anchor the narrative; validation and measurement are shown as part of the operating mechanism rather than a governance appendix; sections move between narrative and product proof.

**Do not copy:** commercial speed/ROI claims, production-readiness language, model-training claims.

**Best for:** Evidence, evaluation, operating conditions, portfolio/value.

---

#### B5 — Weave Bio

Source: https://www.weave.bio/about/

**Capture target:** platform / regulatory-knowledge lifecycle sections if available during review.

**Learn:** human expert ownership can remain visually central in high-stakes AI-enabled knowledge work; lifecycle knowledge, review and reuse can be one coherent product story.

**Do not copy:** pharma-specific workflow, regulatory claims or lifecycle terminology as CLM fact.

**Best for:** Knowledge, authority boundary, evidence lifecycle.

---

### Tier 3 — editorial trust and pacing references

#### C1 — Atticus

Source: https://www.atticus.com/

**Capture target:** homepage editorial sections and explanatory/legal boundary treatment.

**Learn:** serious high-stakes material can be readable and confident; qualifiers can be primary information rather than footnotes; illustration and editorial typography can reduce intimidation without becoming casual.

**Do not copy:** consumer/legal marketing patterns, testimonial/qualification funnel, serif branding as an SEB font decision.

**Best for:** Overview copy/qualifiers; source/evidence explanations.

---

### Tier 4 — research libraries: use as search engines for taste, not as products to imitate

#### D1 — Land-book

Source: https://land-book.com/

Useful filters include industry, style, type and typography.

**Learn:** search for macro-composition references deliberately: editorial + light + illustration; finance/tech + visible borders; resource/software + big type; unusual layout without marketing excess.

**Use for:** 2–3 macro-composition references per golden scene.

---

#### D2 — Lapa Ninja

Source: https://www.lapa.ninja/

Lapa provides full-page screenshots and recordings.

**Learn:** page rhythm and pacing across an entire long page, not only hero design. Study sequences such as large → quiet → dense → visual reset → comparison → closing.

**Use for:** full-page pacing after golden scenes are accepted.

---

#### D3 — Mobbin

Source: https://mobbin.com/

**Learn:** shipped product ergonomics, search, inspectors, source/object detail, filters, history, evidence review and other analytical workspace patterns.

**Use for:** later propagation only; not the primary art direction.

---

#### D4 — SaaSFrame

Source: https://www.saasframe.io/

**Learn:** bridge between website composition and product UI; inspect responsive desktop/mobile product surfaces and flows.

**Use for:** later source/search/object workspace refinement.

---

#### D5 — Page Flows

Source: https://pageflows.com/screens/desktop/saas/

**Learn:** state transitions and user journeys. Use screen recordings/annotations to study what happens after selection, filtering, review or navigation.

**Use for:** interaction behaviour, not global visual style.

---

#### D6 — SiteInspire

Source: https://www.siteinspire.com/

**Learn:** high-end editorial/art-direction examples, unusual layouts and typographic composition.

**Use for:** secondary macro-composition research.

---

#### D7 — One Page Love

Sources:
- https://onepagelove.com/inspiration
- https://onepagelove.com/sections

**Learn:** one-page narrative economy, long-scroll composition, section-level references and clean sequencing.

**Use for:** page rhythm; not product workspace ergonomics.

---

#### D8 — Awwwards interaction/illustration references

Sources:
- https://www.awwwards.com/websites/interaction-design/
- https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration_search/interaction-design/

**Learn:** one interaction or spatial technique at a time: reveal, horizontal transition, direct manipulation, cursor/hover, WebGL, scroll-driven scene changes.

**Do not copy:** novelty for its own sake, conversion theatre, preloader theatrics, illegible motion, award-site density.

**Use for:** interaction spice only after the reasoning representation works statically.

## Reference selection rule per golden scene

Use a maximum of **six active references** in one composition task:

- 2 supplied/generated Hub boards;
- 1 macro-composition reference;
- 1 analytical-workspace reference;
- 1 interaction/reference-flow example when interaction is material;
- 1 optional typography/materiality reference.

More references increase stylistic averaging and reduce authorship.

## Capture annotation schema

Every captured image used in a design session should have this metadata beside it:

- `reference_id`
- `source_url`
- `captured_at`
- `scene_relevance`
- `learn_this`
- `do_not_copy`
- `specific_region_of_interest`
- `why_this_reduces_inference_cost`

The point is not resemblance. The point is to make the desired quality and useful mechanism visible to the model.
